About

Hi, I'm Samir, a PhD researcher at the University of Stuttgart working on machine learning for chemistry. I develop generative models, mostly based on flow matching and diffusion, that predict the three-dimensional structures of molecules, such as stable conformers and the transition states of reactions. Two directions I'm particularly excited about are one-shot methods, which generate a structure in a single step instead of many small ones, and physically inspired generative models, which learn from the potential energy surface itself rather than from atomic coordinates alone.

I came to machine learning from chemical engineering, where I started out simulating molecules and materials with molecular dynamics and developing force fields. During an internship and working-student position at BASF, I worked with Ratan Mishra on molecular modelling for cement chemistry and corrosion protection. My first real contact with machine learning came when I used it to parameterise force fields from scarce experimental data. Moving from engineering into AI meant learning a lot from scratch, and honestly, that's still the part I enjoy most.

My PhD is advised by Mathias Niepert (AI) and Johannes Kästner (theoretical chemistry). Transition states of chemical reactions are my main test case, since they are fleeting, rare in data and expensive to compute. During a research stay in Heather Kulik's group at MIT, I studied how well generative models generalise to chemistry outside their training data, work now published in Nature Computational Science. More recently, I've worked on one-shot generative modelling under symmetries, which will be presented at NeurIPS 2026, and on flow matching that is supervised with the curvature of the potential energy surface.

I expect to finish my PhD in June 2027 and I'm open to R&D roles in AI for science as well as postdoctoral positions. If you're working on related problems, I'd be glad to hear from you.
